# About the hospital communication level

The playable story uses named hospital posts in Northern Sumatra. It connects the broader 2004 Indian Ocean tsunami to a small task that a visitor can understand without knowing radio terminology.

## Evidence and its limits

The [ORARI response account by Wyn W. Purwinto, AB2QV](https://www.qsl.net/ab2qv/ares-tsunami.htm) supplies the hospital names and operational background. It credits ORARI contributors. This is a contemporary participant-informed account, not an independently audited traffic log. The [ARRL 2005 QST index](https://www.arrl.org/files/file/News%20and%20Features/QST%20Annual%20Indexes/2005-QST-Index.pdf) lists the related article in the May issue. Those versions are not independent corroboration.

[USGS explains the earthquake and tsunami generation](https://www.usgs.gov/centers/pcmsc/science/tsunami-generation-2004-m91-sumatra-andaman-earthquake). That source supports the disaster context, not the radio operations.

The educational text and source links are bundled in `tsunami.ts`. The playable hospital pair is in North Sumatra province. The later Meulaboh example is in Aceh. The level keeps those places and periods distinct.

The sources do not establish the exact path drawn, a simultaneous outage at both playable posts, a battery configuration, this message, or this acknowledgement. Those are explicitly labelled teaching conditions. No historical power, signal strength, delivery time, patient identity, or number of lives saved is invented.

## Why this interaction

The visitor chooses power and a communication route, then chooses a message. Failure explains the missing dependency. A successful request receives a reply. Completion means that another team has received the need, while transport and medical care remain separate work.

This is a scripted communication exercise. Reusing the RF engine with guessed historical inputs would make invented measurements look authoritative. The existing RF laboratory remains available through the exit button.

The diagram is a schematic. It shows the broken phone route, radio operators at both hospitals, a working repeater and a return acknowledgement. The repeater is an explicit assumption, so the exercise does not suggest that radio needs no infrastructure.

The emotional focus is waiting to be heard. There are no casualty counters, survival scores, graphic disaster images or invented personal testimonies. The final message gives credit to prepared volunteers and the wider relief response.

## Intended audience outcome

A visitor should leave able to explain that amateur radio can provide an alternative when familiar communication routes fail, but needs prepared people, equipment, energy and a usable path. Sending a message and knowing it was received are different outcomes.

The primary entrance is **When phones fail**. The direct demonstration URL ends in `#tsunami`. Replay resets the exercise. Sources remain optional reading so the demonstration can stay short.
